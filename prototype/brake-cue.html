Brake point countdown

A second overlay window that counts 3 · 2 · 1 · BRAKE into each brake point of the loaded reference lap, with the bar filling in step, so you can match when the reference driver got on the brake. It shows the target peak pressure for the zone, then grades your timing: early, good or late (with "very" either side). Every window below is drawn by the renderer the live prototype uses; open it and press N to jump to the next zone.

Anatomy

  1. 1Zone and distance. Which brake zone is next (numbered in lap order) and how far to its brake point.
  2. 2Zone strip. One pip per zone, coloured by your last grade there. This lap's are bright, last lap's are dim, the next zone is ringed.
  3. 3Countdown bar. Three one-count segments filling toward the cap. The last segment and the fill turn red on "1".
  4. 4Cap. The count, then BRAKE in solid red, flashing, at the reference brake point.
  5. 5Target. The zone's reference peak (outlined red, like the graph's reference labels). The gauge fills with your pressure up to the white target line.
  6. 6Timing. The last zone's grade, how far off the reference brake-on you were in seconds and metres (− early, + late), and your peak against the target.

Into a braking zone

Brake timing grades

Graded on the reference lap's clock, so a slow corner and a fast one are judged alike. The window is a setting (default ±0.08 s, about 5 m at 230 km/h); "very" starts at 3× the window. Chevrons point the way the graph does (early is left, behind the car), so the grade reads without colour.

Other cases

On the graph

With Brake points on the graph on: a red ▼ and dashed line at every reference brake point (so the look-ahead shows the next one coming), and under the baseline a short bar in the grade's colour from the reference brake point to where you braked.

Sizes

Behaviour

ClockEverything runs on the reference lap's clock: index at your LapDistPct ÷ Hz. No speed channel needed. If you're slower down the straight the counts stretch, but BRAKE always lands on the reference brake point.
Brake pointWhere the reference brake crosses 5% (the same threshold that finds brake zones and peak labels). Zones below Skip zones below (default 15%) get no countdown, but still take part in matching, so a light dab isn't mistaken for early braking into the next zone.
CountdownStarts Countdown seconds (default 3 s, one count per second) before the brake point, minus Cue early by (default off; up to 0.3 s to allow for reaction). After a short straight it joins part-way, at 2 or 1, with the skipped part hatched. It never runs while the reference is still braking for the zone before.
Your brake-onYour brake crossing 5%. It's matched to the first zone not yet over, if it's after the previous zone ended and no more than 4 s before the brake point. The first brake-on in a zone counts. Known gap: trail-braking without release through two reference zones (a chicane) reads as No brake for the second.
GradeΔ = your brake-on − reference brake-on, in reference seconds (the metres beside it are for landmarks). Past the brake point without braking, the grade counts up live (outlined chip) until you brake. A zone driven through without braking is No brake.
PeakYour highest pressure in the zone against the reference peak. The difference shows once you're off the brake.
HoldThe timing row keeps the last zone's result until the next zone is decided. The zone strip keeps one result per zone for this lap and the last.
SoundOptional: a short beep on each count and a long, higher one on BRAKE.
SettingsSettings → Brakes (the Labels tab, renamed): countdown window on/off, countdown length, cue early by, skip zones below, beeps; good window; brake points on the graph; then the existing peak label settings.
